BattleTech Starter Sets: Which One Should You Buy?

By David WhiteUpdated 24 September 2026First published 3 May 2026

Catalyst Game Labs makes three starter products and the right choice depends on where you're starting from. Here's a direct breakdown. Note: Catalyst fully refit its entry-level lineup in August 2026, retiring the old Beginner Box and A Game of Armored Combat in favour of a new Starter Box and Core Box, both set in the ilClan era rather than the old Succession Wars starter fluff. This page reflects the current lineup.

The Starter Box — $29.99

Two plastic 'Mechs, the medium Hammerhead and the never-before-in-plastic Kontio, quick-start rules, one double-sided map sheet, MechWarrior skill cards, dice, and the basics you need for a first game. It replaced the old Beginner Box and BattleTech Essentials outright in August 2026, and it's built the same way those were: get playing in minutes, then hand you half-page record sheets whose reverse side previews the fuller rules waiting in the Core Box.

The limitation is the same as it's always been for this tier: two 'Mechs. After a few games you've exhausted what you can do with two units. It's a genuine introduction, not a complete game. Think of it as a demo kit, same as its predecessor.

Buy it if: You want to try BattleTech with minimal investment before committing. Good as a gift for someone who might be interested. Less good if you're already sold on the concept and want to play properly.

The Core Box — $69.99

Eight plastic 'Mechs, the new Core Rulebook, two double-sided maps, a lore booklet called Sagas, MechWarrior cards, dice, and full record sheets. It replaced both A Game of Armored Combat and the separate Clan Invasion set in August 2026, and it's built explicitly on AGoAC's success as its foundation. This is still the right starting point for most people, it's just a different eight 'Mechs now.

The roster changed completely: two per weight class, pulled from across BattleTech's history rather than the old 3025-era classics. Lights are the Solitaire and Hollander, mediums the Eris and Uziel, heavies the Rakshasa and Vulture Mk IV, assaults the Regent and Mad Cat Mk II. None of them are designs this site has a guide for yet, so if you're coming from an older starter box, don't expect the Locust-to-Battlemaster lineup you might have read about elsewhere, that box doesn't exist anymore.

The rules are complete, not simplified or abbreviated. This is the full game, and it's also set in the ilClan era rather than the old Succession Wars default, so you're learning with Clan-tech options in the mix from day one.

Buy it if: You want to actually play BattleTech, not just try it. This is where almost everyone should start.

The Alpha Strike Box Set — Around $60

Thirteen plastic 'Mechs (eight Inner Sphere, five Clan), Alpha Strike quickstart rules, maps, and game aids. A better 'Mech-to-dollar ratio than the Core Box, and Alpha Strike is a legitimate ruleset for faster, larger games. But Alpha Strike is BattleTech's streamlined system, not the original game — and starting there means missing the heat management and detailed record-keeping that define Classic BattleTech's tactical depth.

Buy it if: You know you want Alpha Strike specifically — you want faster games, larger forces, or you're introducing the game to people who won't engage with Classic's complexity. Less suitable as a first purchase if you don't already know which ruleset you prefer.

What to Buy After the Starter

Once you have the Core Box, the next purchases depend on what you want more of. Force packs add four to six 'Mechs for $25–35 and let you build different lance compositions. Some older packs still carry classic 3025-era designs like the Locust or Thunderbolt if you want to mix eras. Free record sheets for any 'Mech are available on the Master Unit List at masterunitlist.info — print what you need rather than buying record sheet books.
