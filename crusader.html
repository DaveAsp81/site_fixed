Crusader

65 tons · Inner Sphere · 28 variants · 2649 to 3147

There are four old Star League heavies I keep coming back to on this site: the Warhammer, the Marauder, the Thunderbolt and this one. We've already covered the other three. The Crusader finishes the set.

It never really had one trick that made people point at it and say that's why we need this thing. It wasn't the fastest heavy, didn't hit the hardest, and there was always something newer or more specialised sitting nearby. It just kept being useful. Close assault, anti-aircraft work, counterinsurgency, reconnaissance, whatever was on the order sheet that week, the Crusader could usually do it without somebody having to drag it back into the bay and rebuild half the machine first. I still reckon that's harder to get right than building something brilliant at one job. Probably explains why factories on Tharkad, Oliver, Asuncion and Bernardo kept turning them out long after the Star League itself was gone.

And the forearms tell you almost everything you need to know about how the Crusader ended up looking the way it does. Early machines were putting too much stress through the arm actuators and structure, with the missile exhaust and heat from the arm-mounted weapons not helping matters. So the engineers added those big barrel-shaped collars around the forearms to take the strain. The fix worked, so it stayed. Then it stayed for centuries. I've had a few of these in the bay over the years and honestly, if the thing has forearms like Popeye, it's probably a Crusader. Nobody set out to make it look like that. Something was breaking, they fixed it, and the fix became part of the machine.

What it is. Sixty-five tons, usually walking four and running six. The standard machine doesn't jump, although variants started sprouting jump jets fairly early, the 3L was doing 120 metres by 2753. Brigadier built the originals in 2596, and over the centuries Kallon, TharHes and others kept the design alive. 28 configurations on record now, the earliest in this catalogue dated 2649, stretching through to 3147.

Why it earns its tonnage

Sixty-five tons, walk four, run six, and a weapons bay built around paired missile racks in the torso and forearms from the very first production run. The forearm baffles that make this chassis instantly recognisable started life as a fix for a real engineering problem, not a styling choice, and that's most of why it still reads as a Crusader at a glance four centuries later.

The CRD-10S, the most recent configuration in the record, posts 7/7/3 on the Alpha Strike card, the best numbers this chassis has ever carried, at zero overheat. The original CRD-2R from 2649 manages a more modest spread. That gap is the whole story of twenty-eight configurations: a design that never stopped being good enough to keep building, and occasionally got good enough to stop apologising for it.

What never changed. Even through MML refits, Light PPC swaps, a Heavy Gauss experiment and a straight twelve-medium-laser brawler build, the chassis kept coming back to paired launchers doing the talking at range. The CRD-8S and CRD-9S, built around a Heavy Gauss Rifle and an Improved Heavy Gauss Rifle respectively, are the real exceptions, and both rank among the strongest configurations here specifically because they broke the pattern.

The quiet fourth member of a famous quartet

Brigadier Corporation built the originals in 2596. The exact configuration of that first production Crusader has never been published, which tells you something about how long ago this chassis actually started. It was designed as a close-assault machine for the Star League Defense Force, and adaptable enough from day one that it ended up doing anti-aircraft work, counterinsurgency and reconnaissance on the same frame without complaint. Factories on Tharkad, Oliver, Asuncion and Bernardo kept it in production straight through the fall of the Star League and into the Succession Wars that followed, which is most of why it's still common enough in 3147 to be getting Dark Age refits.

Kallon Industries ran Crusader lines of its own, components out of Asuncion and Bernardo among them, alongside Brigadier's own continuing production. The Clan Invasion forced the usual wave of upgrades, and the names attached to them tell you who still cared about this chassis forty years after the Star League fell: Cosby BattleMech Research Firm (better known for the No-Dachi), Bander BattleMechs building for the mercenary market, TharHes Industries on Tharkad building for FedCom, and House-specific production runs for the Free Worlds League and the Draconis Combine. A design doesn't get that much continued factory attention from that many different producers across five centuries unless it's actually still earning its keep.

How these are rated

Every verdict below is against other Crusader configurations. Unlike the Clan OmniMechs on this site, these aren't pod-swappable, armour and structure vary by refit, so a config here can genuinely be strictly worse than another and the Trap tier applies properly.

Worth it

Nothing in its era does the chassis's job better for the money.

Solid

Does the job competently. Another variant does it better or cheaper, but there is no reason to avoid this one.

Situational

Only earns its Battle Value if you specifically need what it carries. C3, stealth, a command console, flak, jump jets.

Trap

Another variant in the same era, at no more than 3 percent above its Battle Value, matches or beats it on every Alpha Strike damage band, on armour, on structure, and carries every special ability it has. Nothing gained, something lost, no dearer.

Clan-tech double heat sinks and a Clan XL engine turn in the best numbers this chassis has ever posted: seven points of damage at both short and medium range, zero overheat. Four hundred years after the original, this is still clearly a Crusader, and it finally fights like one that knows it.

Worth it

An Improved Heavy Gauss Rifle anchors this one, for the best long-range damage of any Crusader built, at zero overheat. If you want the chassis to out-range what it used to just out-missile, this is the configuration.

Worth it

65 tons · BV 1698 · PV 34 · Skirmisher · 3066
Walk 4 / Run 6 / Jump 4 · 10 IS Double · 260 XL Engine
2x LRM 10, 2x Medium Laser, 1x Heavy Gauss Rifle

A single Heavy Gauss Rifle replaces most of the missile battery, for real long-range bite on an XL engine and jump 4, zero overheat. A genuine departure from the chassis's missile-boat identity, and a good one.

6T: twelve medium lasers, nothing else

Worth it

65 tons · BV 1890 · PV 49 · Skirmisher · 3053
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 17 IS Double · 325 XL Engine
2x Flamer, 12x Medium Laser

Twelve medium lasers and nothing else, on jump 5 and a C3 slave. Six points of damage at short range, zero past medium. Built to close distance and empty the whole battery in one alpha.

4BR: the best of the Star League tech refits

Worth it

65 tons · BV 1407 · PV 37 · Missile Boat · 3054
Walk 4 / Run 6 / Jump 0 · 10 IS Double · 260 Fusion Engine
2x Streak SRM 2, 2x LRM 15, 2x Medium Laser, 2x Machine Gun

Bander BattleMechs' mercenary-market retrofit: double heat sinks, Streak SRM 2 in place of the old SRM 6, zero overheat, improved indirect fire. The best of the Star League tech refits for the price.

On the table

Fielding one. Know which era of Crusader you're holding before you commit it. The early Star League missile boats want to sit back and let the LRMs work. The later Heavy Gauss and PPC refits want to actually use their reach. Fielding a CRD-2R like it's a CRD-9S will get the range band wrong.

Opposing one. At 1204 to 2264 Battle Value, the spread is wide enough that you genuinely need to know which configuration you're facing. A 6T with twelve medium lasers wants to close distance fast; let it and you'll feel it. Most of the rest are missile boats that would rather you stayed at range, so closing the gap against those specifically is usually the right call.

Against OpFor Command

If you run solo games with our AI opponent, the Crusader is a genuinely mixed bag, which makes it a useful one to field when you want the AI's behaviour to actually vary between engagements.

  • Most configurations are tagged Missile Boat, so expect the engine to hang back and let indirect fire do the early work.
  • The 6T and a handful of Skirmisher-tagged refits behave completely differently, closing distance and brawling. Don't assume you know the AI's plan just because you know the chassis.

Common questions

What makes a Crusader easy to identify?

The barrel-shaped baffles on the forearms. They were originally added to vent heat and exhaust fumes from the forearm-mounted missile launchers, which were damaging the actuators without them. The fix became the chassis's defining silhouette, sometimes nicknamed "Popeye forearms."

Who manufactures the Crusader?

Brigadier Corporation built the original design for the Star League Defense Force. Later refits came from Cosby BattleMech and Myomer Research Firm, TharHes Industries, and several House-specific producers across the Succession Wars era.

What is the best Crusader configuration?

The CRD-10S for the best raw numbers the chassis has ever posted, the CRD-9S for the best long-range damage via its Improved Heavy Gauss Rifle, and the CRD-4BR for the best value among the classic Star League tech refits.

Is the Crusader a missile boat?

Most configurations are, built around paired LRM and SRM launchers in the torso and forearms. A handful of later refits, the CRD-8S and CRD-9S with their Heavy Gauss Rifles and the CRD-6T with twelve medium lasers, break from that completely.
