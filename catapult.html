Catapult

65 tons · Inner Sphere · 19 variants · 2561 to 3136

The Star League tried the Catapult for three years and decided not to keep it. It did the job, the review said, it just wasn't quite what they wanted.

Hollis built it from 2561 to 2563 on a limited contract for the Terran Hegemony, their first ever BattleMech. When the contract came up for renewal the Hegemony passed, and Hollis switched the factory over to the BattleMaster. The Catapults already built drifted into second-line and mountain units and slowly wore out.

Then the Star League fell, the last factory went with it, and the Successor States started invading each other specifically to capture the survivors. A mech nobody wanted became one people went to war over. I don't think any other design on this site has a turnaround quite like it.

What you've got. Two LRM 15s in those ears, four medium lasers under the cockpit, four jump jets, ten tons of armour and 15 heat sinks. No arms at all, which the quirk list states outright. It's built to stand well back and drop missiles on things from six hundred metres.

The two things that bite you. Ammunition, first: eight reloads per launcher and then you're down to lasers. And the head: weak head armour is on the official quirk list, same as the BattleMaster and the Timber Wolf. Keep it back and screened and neither matters much. Put it in a brawl and both will.

If you're choosing one: the CPLT-C1 is the classic, the CPLT-C1b fixes its ammunition and armour, the CPLT-K2 turns it into a PPC boat, and the CPLT-C5 is the artillery version that doesn't run dry.

Why it earns its tonnage

The shape tells you what it's for. The missile racks sit up in the ears, the lasers tuck under the cockpit, and there are no arms at all. It's a missile platform on a pair of legs and a set of jump jets, and everything about it assumes someone else is holding the front line.

That's fine as long as it's true. Two LRM 15s from long range, a jump to the next bit of cover, repeat. Four medium lasers mean a light mech that runs in to finish it gets a nasty surprise. The problem is endurance. Eight reloads per launcher goes quickly, and once the missiles are gone you're a sixty-five ton machine with four mediums and not much armour.

Two bits of engineering history worth knowing if you ever get one in the bay. The original Anderson 21 jump jets had a habit of breaking down and venting exhaust into the mech, which cooked it from inside. There was a general recall in 2566 to fit Anderson 25s, and some Catapults never got the refit. And the ejection system fires sideways. Perfectly safe, apparently, but a genuine surprise the first time.

At range the C1 runs cool for as long as the missiles last. The heat only turns up when something's close enough for the lasers, which is exactly when you least want to be slowed down.

Turned down, then fought over

After the Hegemony let the contract lapse, the remaining Catapults stayed in service with specialist and second-line units, mountaineering regiments among them, and Hollis made just enough spare parts to keep a shrinking number of them walking.

When the Star League fell, a lot of the survivors went with Kerensky on the Exodus. The Capellan Confederation gathered up most of the ones left behind, and the Draconis Combine took a sizeable batch when it captured Dieron. Hollis's factory on Corey briefly started making new ones and spare parts again, and then it was destroyed at the start of the First Succession War.

That's when it got strange. With no factory anywhere, the Catapult became rare enough to be worth fighting over, and there were whole invasions launched simply to seize the remaining machines, the Federated Suns' campaigns against the Confederation above all.

It took until 3033 for things to turn round, when the Combine contracted Yori 'Mech Works to restart production of the K2 on Al Na'ir. The C3 artillery variant followed, and paired with Ravens for spotting it became one of the more feared combinations of its era, despite nearly five centuries separating the two designs. By the Clan Invasion the Confederation and the Combine held most of the Catapults in existence, and new variants kept coming for decades.

And one last thing: it's half the reason the Timber Wolf is called the Mad Cat. When an Inner Sphere targeting computer first met one, it couldn't decide between a Marauder and a Catapult.

On the table

Piloting one. Stay back, stay in cover, and jump between firing positions rather than walking between them. Count your reloads from turn one, because eight per launcher runs out faster than you'd expect. Keep the lasers for anything that reaches you, and don't start a fight where they're your main weapon.

Fighting one. Close the distance. Inside its minimum range the LRMs are wasted, and it has no arms to fight with. Aim for the head if you get the chance, since the weak head armour quirk is working for you. And if it's the C1 or A1, you can simply outlast its ammunition.

What I see go wrong. Catapults getting put in the front rank because they're sixty-five tons and look sturdy. They aren't. It's a support mech with a heavy's weight, and it needs someone standing in front of it.

Against OpFor Command

If you run solo games with our AI opponent, the Catapult splits three ways: 10 Missile Boats, 5 Snipers and 4 Skirmishers.

  • The C1 family are Missile Boats and will hang right back, which is what they should do.
  • The K-series PPC variants play as Snipers and will trade shots at range.
  • Butterbee, the K5, the H2 and the C6 are Skirmishers and will close in, which suits their short-range loadouts.

16 of the 19 variants carry jump jets, so expect an enemy Catapult to move between firing positions rather than stand still.

Common questions

What is the best Catapult variant?

The CPLT-C1 at BV 1399 is the classic. The C1b fixes its ammunition and armour problems, the K2 and K2K turn it into a PPC direct-fire mech, and the C5 is the artillery version with enough ammunition to last. No variant fails the Trap test.

Why was the Catapult so rare during the Succession Wars?

The Star League only built it from 2561 to 2563 before letting the contract lapse, and the Corey plant that later tried to restart production was lost when the First Succession War opened. Catapults became valuable enough that whole invasions were launched just to capture the remaining ones.

What are the Catapult’s weaknesses?

Limited ammunition, with eight reloads per launcher on the C1, no arms for close combat, and weak head armour on its quirk list. It's a support mech and does best kept behind a screen.

Is the Catapult part of the Mad Cat name?

Yes. When Inner Sphere forces first met the Timber Wolf, a targeting computer couldn't decide whether it was a Marauder or a Catapult, and the after-action analysis gave it the reporting name Mad Cat.

What does the CPLT-C3 do?

It swaps both LRM 15s for an Arrow IV artillery system, letting it hit targets from well beyond direct line of fire. It worked especially well paired with a Raven for spotting, though it only carries one ton of ammunition. The later C5 fixes that.
