Marauder

75 tons · Inner Sphere · 36 variants · 2612 to 3146

General Motors put the Marauder out in 2612 and it was not supposed to be a classic. It was supposed to be the start of something. A new generation of BattleMechs, attack and direct fire support, built around long range work in a way nothing else was. Then the Star League fell and the generation it was meant to open never turned up. The Marauder is what the future looked like, left standing on its own after the future was cancelled.

It is twelve metres of nothing you would call humanoid, and that was a feature. The silhouette is genuinely harder to hit than the usual upright designs, which is on the record as a quirk of the chassis and not just pilots talking themselves up.

What is actually on it. Two Magna Hellstar particle cannons in armoured gauntlets, same idea as a Warhammer but on ball-and-socket joints, so it traverses faster and covers more ground with its guns. A General Motors Whirlwind autocannon on the right torso throwing three-round bursts of 120mm. Those three guns are the long range set and they are meant to work together. Then two Magna Mk II mediums, also in the gauntlets, which are not part of that set at all. They are what you have left when somebody has closed the distance on you, and the whole design is about making sure they have to.

And the part the brochure skips. 16 single heat sinks on the MAD-3R. Two Hellstars cost 20 heat between them, walking costs one more, and you dissipate 16. The old manuals say a competent MechWarrior keeps the heat under control. What they mean is do not fire both. Two turns of the full pair and you are down three movement points with a to-hit penalty and rolling to stay switched on.

If you are choosing a variant: the MAD-3D throws the autocannon away, adds four sinks and fixes the problem for 107 BV. The MAD-5S is the definitive modern sniper. The MAD-11D is the strongest late one. And the MAD-4X and its binary lasers is a trap no matter how good Blazers look written down.

Why it earns its tonnage

Most heavies in this bracket are built to trade damage inside medium range. The Marauder is not, and you can see it the moment you get the panels off. The two Hellstars and the Whirlwind are built to be used together at long range, which is the whole point of the machine: you are doing damage through the entire approach instead of waiting to arrive somewhere. The autocannon actually reaches further than the particle cannons do, so it opens the account and the Hellstars join in as the range comes down.

The two medium lasers are a separate matter entirely. They are close-in weapons and they are there for the situation the rest of the design exists to prevent. If you are using them, something has gone wrong.

The gauntlets are the clever part. A Warhammer carries its PPCs the same way, but the Marauder's arms run on ball-and-socket joints, so it swings its guns onto a target faster and covers more of the field without moving its feet. The Hellstars are also built tough enough to survive being used as clubs, and there are enough accounts of Marauders beating something to scrap with them that I believe it.

Now the parts I actually swear about.

The Whirlwind was an afterthought. You can tell by how it is attached: the linkage between the autocannon and the chassis is thin, and one good hit in the right place takes the gun out of the fight without touching anything else. It is temperamental at the best of times. It is on the official quirk list as an exposed weapon linkage, which is the polite way of saying what every tech who has worked one already knows.

The rotation rings where the torso meets the leg assemblies are the other weak point, and enough pilots have welded improvised plate around them over the years that you stop being surprised when one comes in with a home-made collar on it.

And the HiRez tracking gear sits forward in the chassis where it blocks part of the pilot's own view, which is a strange thing to do to a machine built around seeing people a long way off.

One more, and it is a sad one. The original armour was Valiant Lamellor, eleven and a half tons of it, better than standard plate at spreading both kinetic and thermal energy and lighter with it. Nobody remembers how to make it. Plenty of them have had sections replaced with whatever was going, so a lot of the older ones are patchwork now. You can read a machine's whole service history off its plating if you know what you are looking at.

The heat maths nobody tells you

The MAD-3R runs 16 single heat sinks. Two Hellstars cost 20 heat. Walking costs one more. That is 21 against 16 dissipation, so you are up five a turn doing the thing the machine exists to do.

TurnActionEnd heatWhat it costs you
1Both PPCs, walk5Minus one walking MP
2Both PPCs, walk10Minus two MP, plus one to hit
3Both PPCs, walk15Minus three MP, plus two to hit, shutdown checks

So it is two on, one off, or one Hellstar plus the autocannon and lasers as your sustainable output. Decide which before the game rather than on turn three. It is also the best single argument for the MAD-3D: it drops the troublesome Whirlwind entirely, puts four more heat sinks in the space, and lands within a point of heat neutral on the same guns for 107 more BV.

Is it better than a Warhammer?

This is the comparison that actually matters in a 3025 game, because both are twin-PPC platforms in the same bracket. There is a full Warhammer write-up if you want the other side of it. Straight off the data: the Warhammer WHM-6R is 70 tons at BV 1299 with 18 single heat sinks. The Marauder MAD-3R is 75 tons at BV 1363 with 16 single heat sinks.

So the Warhammer is cheaper, runs cooler, and carries an SRM 6 for close work. The Marauder is better armoured (6 against 5 on the Alpha Strike card) and hits harder at long range. That is the trade. If you want a twin-PPC mech that can keep shooting, take the Warhammer. If you want one that can absorb the return fire and reach further out, take the Marauder.

Worth naming the third option too. The Black Knight BL-6-KNT is the same 75 tons at BV 1551 with 20 single heat sinks and no ammunition anywhere on the chassis. It is the better sustained energy platform. It just cannot match the Marauder's reach.

Six hundred years of not quite arriving

In the Star League years it was a Gunslinger Program favourite, because of the firepower, and battalion and regimental commanders liked it for its electronics. It was never common. Massed Marauders broke enemy lines properly when anyone could gather enough of them, and almost nobody could. House Liao fielded several hundred in total in 2828. That is the whole Confederation, all of them, a few hundred machines. So they got paired out with Warhammers and Thunderbolts to make up the numbers, which is why those three keep turning up together in accounts of the Succession Wars.

Then the decline, and this is the part the variant list tells better than any history book. The MAD-2R from 2760 had ER PPCs and 16 double heat sinks. It fires both main guns every turn, forever, and never notices. 59 years later they built the MAD-3R with single sinks, standard cannons and less armour, and that is the one on the box art. It took another 287 years before anyone put double heat sinks back into a production Marauder.

When the Helm Memory Core turned up, the Marauder was an obvious thing to modernise. House Davion got there first and then made the most Davion mistake available: House Kurita took Quentin and the Marauder factory on it during the War of 3039, which handed the Combine the new variant. The Federated Commonwealth had to go away and design a second one with a different loadout because the enemy owned the first.

The Capellans came to it late and sideways. They had never built a Marauder in their lives. Sun-Tzu Liao negotiated with General Motors personally for the rights, and what he traded was permission for HildCo Interplanetary to start exporting to the Federated Suns again. It worked. And not long after, reports started coming out of the Periphery about backwater realms fielding upgraded Marauders that nobody could account for.

How these are rated

Every verdict below is against other Marauders available in the same era, not against the rest of the game. Whether a Marauder beats a Warhammer is a real question and it has its own section above.

Worth it

Nothing in its era does the chassis's job better for the money.

Solid

Does the job competently. Another variant does it better or cheaper, but there is no reason to avoid this one.

Situational

Only earns its Battle Value if you specifically need what it carries. C3, stealth, a command console, flak, jump jets.

Trap

Another variant in the same era, at no more than 3 percent above its Battle Value, matches or beats it on every Alpha Strike damage band, on armour, on structure, and carries every special ability it has. Nothing gained, something lost, no dearer.

Only the Trap tier is calculated. It is worked out from the variant data rather than decided by me, which means it is falsifiable: to argue with it you only have to name something the cheaper machine gives up. MAD-4X: the trap

Trap

75 tons · BV 1453 · PV 33 · Brawler · 3039
Walk 4 / Run 6 / Jump 0 · 18 Single · 300 Fusion Engine
2x Binary Laser (Blazer) Cannon, 2x SRM 6

Binary laser cannons. Enormous heat for large laser damage, and no long-range punch at all on the Alpha Strike card. The experiment that proves why nobody else fitted Blazers. Beaten outright by the MAD-SD (Douglass), which costs 6 more BV but gives up nothing it carries.

On the table

Piloting one. The Marauder wants a long firing lane and a reason for the enemy to come to it. Pick your ground first, because at 4/6 you are not repositioning out of a bad decision. Keep one PPC in reserve on hot turns rather than alphaing and then spending two turns useless. Partial cover suits it well given the armour already on the chassis.

Fighting one. Close the distance and make it move. Every hex it walks is heat it cannot spend on guns, and inside short range the twin PPC fit is doing less work than almost anything it is facing. The arms are the target: both main guns are mounted there, so an arm hit takes half the mech's output off the field. Against XL-engined fits from the Clan Invasion onward, the side torso is the kill.

Common mistakes. Running it forward with the rest of the lance, treating the autocannon as a real weapon rather than incidental damage, and alphaing on turn one. The 3R in particular punishes all three.

Against OpFor Command

Worth knowing if you run solo games with our AI opponent: the Marauder does not have one behaviour, it has several, because the engine reads each variant's role tag rather than the chassis name.

  • The MAD-3R and the other Sniper fits are weighted toward long range and given a negative aggression modifier. They hold distance, favour clean shots, and will not walk into a brawl to take one.
  • The MAD-3D, MAD-7D and the other Brawler fits are weighted the opposite way and get a strong positive aggression modifier. Same chassis, and they will come at you.
  • Skirmisher fits like the MAD-5D sit in the middle and prioritise targets that are already damaged or cut off from support.

The practical upshot is that swapping a 3R for a 3D in an enemy lance changes the whole shape of the game, not just the damage numbers. If a scenario is feeling static, that is the dial to turn.

Common questions

What is the best Marauder variant?

It depends on era. Before the Clans arrive, the MAD-3D at BV 1470 solves the heat problem that limits the 3R. The MAD-2R at BV 1630 is better still if Star League tech is on the table. For the Clan Invasion and later, the MAD-5S is the definitive sniper fit, and the MAD-11D is the strongest Dark Age option.

Why does the Marauder overheat?

The MAD-3R carries 16 single heat sinks and its two PPCs generate 20 heat between them. Add a point for walking and you are running a five point surplus every turn you fire both. The Star League MAD-2R had double heat sinks and does not have this problem at all.

What is the difference between the Marauder, the Marauder II and the Marauder IIC?

The Marauder is the 75 ton original. The Marauder II is a 100 ton assault-weight rebuild on the same silhouette. The Marauder IIC is Clan Wolf's 85 ton version built on Clan technology, and it is the most dangerous of the three. There are 65 variants across the whole family.

Which factions can field a Marauder?

The 3R is close to universal through the Succession Wars, appearing across the Inner Sphere, the Periphery and the major mercenary commands. Late-era variants narrow considerably. The table above has the full per-era breakdown.

Is the Marauder good for new players?

Yes, with one caveat. It is forgiving in the sense that it is well armoured and does not ask you to make fast movement decisions. It is unforgiving in that the heat management is real and you will feel it in your first game. That is arguably a good thing to learn early.
