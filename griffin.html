Griffin

55 tons · Inner Sphere · 24 variants · 2465 to 3144

Earthwerks put the production Griffin out in 2492, after Maxwell Manufacturing had been folded into the company. It was drawn up as a heavy. The weight classes got rewritten underneath it, and it has been a medium ever since, still doing the job on the original drawing: stand somewhere awkward and hit things that cannot comfortably hit back.

There is an earlier machine in the list, the GRF-1A of 2465, and it weighs sixty tons because it is a primitive. Nobody means that one when they say Griffin.

What you've got, on the machine everyone actually fields, is one PPC, one LRM 10, five jump jets and twelve single heat sinks. Walk five, run eight. A standard 275, which matters more than it looks once the later fits start swapping the engine out.

The first time I really learned a mech it was one of these, on a ridge in a MechWarrior 2 intro, and the lesson has not moved. The jump jets are how you get there. They are not how you fight once you have arrived.

If you're choosing one: the GRF-1N is the Griffin, and it is still the right answer in a Succession Wars game. The GRF-3M is the Clan Invasion pick. The GRF-6S is the one with the better guns, and the GRF-3N is the one that kept the engine. The Griffin C is what you buy when the bill has stopped mattering.

Why it earns its tonnage

The PPC reaches. The LRM 10 reaches too, and it can fire indirectly, which is the IF1 on the Alpha Strike card and the reason a Griffin behind a hill is still working. Five jump jets put fifty-five tons on top of the terrain feature everyone else has to walk around. Nine and a half tons of armour is an honest amount for the weight. None of this is clever. It is why the design outlasted the paperwork that reclassified it.

Two tons of LRM ammunition is the only thing on the original that can explode. Everything else is energy and metal. Lose the right arm and the PPC is gone. Lose a side torso on the 1N and the machine is hurt, not dead, because the engine is a standard 275. A lot of the later variants give that second fact away to fit a bigger missile or a lighter power plant. Read the engine line before you fall in love with the guns.

The thing the record sheet does not tell you is that the three tricks are not meant to happen on the same turn.

The heat maths on the GRF-1N

Twelve single heat sinks. A PPC is 10 heat. An LRM 10 is 4. Jumping costs 1 heat per hex, minimum 3, and it replaces the walking heat rather than stacking on top of it. The columns below are the change from whatever you started the turn on. Heat does not go below zero.

What you didHeat inSinksChange
Stand still, both guns1412+2
Walk, both guns1512+3
Run, both guns1612+4
Jump 5, LRM only912−3
Jump 5, PPC only1512+3
Jump 5, both guns1912+7

The missile is what you fire while you are still moving. The PPC is what you fire when you have arrived. Both, on a full jump, puts you up seven. From a cold start that is already a movement penalty, and it is not yet a gunnery penalty. Do it again and you finish on fourteen. The accurate long-range machine you climbed the hill to be is now the thing missing.

Standing and firing both every turn is not free either. It is plus two a turn. You get away with it twice. The third time leaves you on six, and the movement penalty is there the next time you want to leave. The sustainable pattern is boring and correct: both guns when you are cold and planted, PPC only when you are warm, LRM only when you had to jump. The doubles on the later machines buy a lot of this back. They do not repeal it. An ER PPC is 15 heat before you have moved.

Is it better than a Shadow Hawk?

Different question from which Griffin, and worth keeping separate. In the 3025 game most people actually play, the three fifty-five tonners that share a lance are not three answers to the same problem.

The Shadow Hawk SHD-2H is BV 1064, damage 2/2/1, jump three. The Wolverine WVR-6R is BV 1101, damage 2/2/1, jump five. The GRF-1N is BV 1272, damage 1/2/2, jump five. You are paying 208 more than the Hawk and 171 more than the Wolverine, and what you are buying is the long bracket, plus a gun that does not need the ammunition the Hawk is terrified of. What you are giving up is a point of short-range damage and, against the Wolverine, the ability to stand in the middle of a fight without a plan.

So no, it is not a better general medium. It is a better sniper, and the sniper is the job. If all you wanted was jumping missiles, the Dervish DV-6M does that for BV 1146 at 2/2/2 and gives up the PPC and a point of armour. The Vindicator VND-1R is the smaller, slower version of the same idea, BV 1024 at 2/2/2, with an LRM 5 and a 4/6/4 movement curve. Take the Griffin when the hill, and the reach from it, is the plan. Take the Wolverine when you do not yet know what the plan is.

A heavy, reclassified, then everybody's

The primitive GRF-1A is the tell. Sixty tons, a prototype PPC, an LRM 5, jump three, introduced in 2465. The production article in 2492 lost five tons, gained a real PPC and a real LRM 10, and gained two more hexes of jump. Then the weight-class definitions caught up and called the result a medium. The job description never changed. Stand off. Shoot. Leave if the map stops agreeing with you.

After that it stopped belonging to anyone. Earthwerks built it on Keystone. Defiance built it on Hesperus II. Kallon built it on Talon. Brigadier built it on Oliver, Victory on Marduk, Vandenberg on Illiushin. The Capellans mostly bought a limited number off the Taurians rather than tooling up themselves, which is as good a picture as any of a design that had already escaped its first factory. By the late Succession Wars a Griffin in a militia lance was not a story. The absence of one was.

The Royal, the GRF-2N of 2751, is not the improved sniper people assume a Star League Griffin must be. It swaps the LRM 10 for a pair of SRM 6s, fits an ER PPC, ECM and CASE, and the Alpha Strike card comes out 3/3/1. Better up close, worse at the thing the chassis is for, and 334 Battle Value above the 1N. Production of it did not survive the fall. What the Master Unit List still shows afterwards is leftover machines: ComStar, a scatter of houses early in the war, and the Clans, who kept theirs in second-line formations. You do not bump into a 2N at a militia depot.

The Lyran GRF-1S took the PPC out entirely and lived with a large laser, which tells you what Lyran quartermasters thought of the heat. The Helm Core and the Clan Invasion then did what they did to everything else. The GRF-3M put an ER PPC next to an LRM 20 and paid for it with an XL engine. Project Phoenix, the Vicore redesign, produced the GRF-6S, built by Defiance and Kallon and later Victory, and that is the first late Griffin that feels like a finished version of the original idea rather than a refit done in a hurry.

Two of them are people. Sparky was Elle Bennett's machine in the Fifth Donegal Guards, the LRM pulled for five medium lasers. Francine was a Griffin brought up to 6S standard and used in the fight to get the Black Widow Company off Royal Pass in 3070, then pushed further into experimental gear. They are in the table because they are real variants. They are not a production plan.

How these are rated

Every verdict below is against other Griffins available in the same era. Not against the Shadow Hawk, and not against the rest of the game. That second question is the section above.

Worth it

Nothing in its era does the chassis's job better for the money.

Solid

Does the job competently. Another variant does it better or cheaper, but there is no reason to avoid this one.

Situational

Only earns its Battle Value if you specifically need what it carries. C3, stealth, a command console, flak, jump jets.

Trap

Another variant in the same era, at no more than 3 percent above its Battle Value, matches or beats it on every Alpha Strike damage band, on armour, on structure, and carries every special ability it has. Nothing gained, something lost, no dearer.

Only the Trap tier is calculated. It is worked out from the variant data rather than decided by me, which means it is falsifiable: to argue with it you only have to name something the cheaper machine gives up. The other three are my opinion and you should treat them that way. 0 of the 24 Griffin variants fail the Trap test. The full rating scale is here, including what it deliberately does not measure.

Zero, on a list this long, is the interesting result. The variants keep changing the job instead of doing the same job worse. A laser boat with the jets pulled, a stealth armour fit, a C3 node, a sixty-ton primitive. None of those is a strictly worse 1N. They are different machines that happen to share a name.

The closest the test came was the GRF-3RG against the GRF-1RG. Same damage bands, same refusal to jump, CASE on the newer one, and forty-six Battle Value between them. Forty-six is more than three percent of 1167, so the old laser boat does not fail. I checked, because I wanted it to.

The variants worth arguing about

GRF-1A: the heavy that didn't last

Situational

The prototype, and the only Griffin that isn't fifty-five tons. Primitive components, a prototype PPC, an LRM 5, jump three. Field it when the scenario is the Age of War and the machine is supposed to be obsolete. Fielding it next to a 1N, in any era they share, is how you pay for a history lesson.

GRF-1N: the one on the ridge

Worth it

55 tons · BV 1272 · PV 31 · Sniper · 2492
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 12 Single · 275 Fusion Engine
1x PPC, 1x LRM 10

The icon, and for once the icon is also the correct buy. One PPC, one LRM 10, jump five, a standard engine, BV 1272. Nothing else in the Succession Wars does this job for less, and the things that do it better show up centuries later and cost more. Learn the heat section before you alpha off a hill. After that it will do what you ask for as long as you ask one thing at a time.

GRF-2N: the Royal, which is not a better sniper

Solid

55 tons · BV 1606 · PV 38 · Skirmisher · 2751
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 11 IS Double · 275 Fusion Engine
1x ER PPC, 2x SRM 6

Star League Royal. ER PPC, two SRM 6s, ECM, CASE, a point more armour, and the damage bands come out 3/3/1. That last number is the whole review. The long bracket got worse. You paid 334 Battle Value over a 1N to win the fight inside medium range and to carry an ECM suite. Take it when you have one and the map is short. Do not take it instead of a 1N and expect the same lane.

GRF-1S: the Lyran who took the PPC out

Solid

55 tons · BV 1253 · PV 31 · Skirmisher · 2857
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 16 Single · 275 Fusion Engine
1x Large Laser, 2x Medium Laser, 1x LRM 5

Lyran Commonwealth, and the PPC is gone. A large laser, two mediums, an LRM 5, and sixteen single heat sinks so the machine can actually shoot while it jumps. Damage 2/2/0. It no longer does the Griffin's job. It does a perfectly reasonable medium's job for nineteen points under the 1N, and a Lyran player in a 3025 game is not wrong to prefer it. Everyone else should notice what was removed.

GRF-3M: the invasion answer

Worth it

55 tons · BV 1521 · PV 33 · Sniper · 3049
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 13 IS Double · 275 XL Engine
1x ER PPC, 1x Small Laser, 1x LRM 20

This is the Griffin people reached for once the Clans were on the map. ER PPC, LRM 20, doubles, and the card goes to 2/3/3. The bill is an XL engine and BV 1521. In Classic that XL is the thing that will kill you: one side torso and the machine is a salvage report. In return you get the long-range profile the 1N was pretending to have. For the Clan Invasion and the Civil War, if you can accept the engine, this is the one.

GRF-6S: Project Phoenix, finished

Worth it

55 tons · BV 1781 · PV 37 · Sniper · 3066
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 10 IS Double · 275 Light Engine
2x ER Medium Laser, 1x ER PPC, 1x LRM 15

The Vicore redesign, and the first late Griffin that feels designed rather than accumulated. ER PPC, LRM 15, two ER medium lasers, a light engine, damage 2/3/3, a point more armour, BV 1781. The light engine is the sentence to read twice. In Classic it is not an XL: lose a side torso and you are still in the fight. The Alpha Strike card in our database lists its structure at 3, the same figure it gives the XL fits, which understates that difference. Believe the engine type, not the coincidence.

GRF-3N: the one that kept the engine

Worth it

55 tons · BV 1560 · PV 34 · Sniper · 3093
Walk 5 / Run 8 / Jump 5 · 11 IS Double · 275 Fusion Engine
1x ER PPC, 1x Enhanced LRM 10

Introduced for the Marik-Stewart Commonwealth and then, on the Master Unit List, everyone else's problem. ER PPC, enhanced LRM 10, CASE II, standard 275, armour 6, structure 5, damage 2/2/2, BV 1560. It gives a damage band back to the 6S and costs 221 less, and it keeps the quality the original had: a side torso is damage, not a kill. From the Republic era on, if you want a Griffin that still behaves like a Griffin when it is losing, this is the one I would sign for.

Griffin C: the bill

Worth it

55 tons · BV 2131 · PV 45 · Sniper · 3144
Walk 6 / Run 9 / Jump 9 · 11 IS Double · 330 XL Engine
1x ER PPC, 1x Streak LRM 10

Dark Age, mixed tech, and the MUL lists it as a general refit rather than one house's private toy. Clan ER PPC, Streak LRM 10, jump nine, damage 3/3/3, BV 2131. Nothing else on the chassis matches the card. The engine is an XL again, so the old weakness is back, and you are paying more than a stock Atlas AS7-D for a fifty-five ton frame. Worth it when the force can field it and the alternative is a worse Griffin. The 6S is still the sane purchase.

The rest of the list

The GRF-1RG and the GRF-3RG pull the jump jets and turn the chassis into a laser boat, damage 4/3/0, no reach at all. Fine if that was the order. Pointless if you wanted a Griffin. The GRF-1DS is the Combine's LRM 20 fit and it works, XL and all. The GRF-5M is a Free Worlds light Gauss that stays cool and hits gently. The GRF-5K is the Combine machine with jump eight and a light PPC, which is the right variant on a map made of hills and the wrong one when you needed the big gun. None of the three embarrasses the name. All three are Solid.

The GRF-5L is Capellan and Canopian stealth. The GRF-6R is the Republic stealth fit with a Thunderbolt missile. The GRF-6CS is ComStar and Word of Blake C3i. The GRF-6S2 carries a C3 slave and a targeting computer. You already know, before you look at the guns, whether your force uses any of that. If it doesn't, you are paying for a hobby. The GRF-4R and the GRF-4N are strikers, faster, and done with the long range. Sparky, Sparky 2.0, Francine, Francine II and the Almstedt are pilots. Sparky 2.0 is the only one of those I would call Solid on the merits. The others are Situational because the story is doing the work the Battle Value isn't.

On the table

Piloting one. Pick the ridge before you need it. At 5/8/5 you can get there, and every hex you jump on the way is heat the PPC wanted. Land, plant, fire both if you are cold. If you have to keep moving, walk, and if you have to jump, fire the LRM and leave the PPC for the turn you stop. Partial cover suits it. The right arm is the gun you cannot afford to gamble. Ammunition is the only explosive on the 1N, so a torso hit is not the religious experience it is on a Shadow Hawk, but it is still the bin.

Fighting one. Make it jump. A Griffin that is jumping is a Griffin that is not shooting both guns, and if you can force that for two turns in a row the heat does the rest. Get inside the range where your short-band guns out-damage a 1/2/2 card. The PPC lives in the arm. Take the arm and the long gun is gone, and what is left is an LRM 10 with ambitions. Against the XL fits, from the 3M and the 1DS onward, ignore the arm and take a side torso. Against the 1N and the 3N you have to actually kill it. The legs are a serious option on any of them. A Griffin that cannot jump is just a slow PPC with a missile pack, and there are cheaper ways to get that.

What I see go wrong. People jump and alpha every turn, hit the teens, and then explain to the table that the Griffin runs hot. It doesn't. The pilot does. The other mistake is treating every variant as the 1N with different decals. A 4R will walk into your face. A 5L will not show up on the sensors you were relying on. Read the engine and the role before you decide where to stand.

Against OpFor Command

If you run solo games with our AI opponent, the Griffin does not have one behaviour. The engine reads the role tag on the variant, and this chassis splits almost in half: 10 Skirmishers and 9 Snipers, with 3 Missile Boats and 2 Strikers making up the rest of the 24.

  • The GRF-1N, the GRF-3M, the GRF-6S and the GRF-3N are Snipers. They hold a lane and they do not come and get you.
  • The GRF-2N and the laser boats play as Skirmisher, and they will spend the movement the snipers hoard.
  • The GRF-1DS and the GRF-5M are Missile Boats. They are happy at the back, and the 1DS in particular will try to keep the LRM 20 in arc rather than use the jump jets for anything heroic.
  • The GRF-4R and the GRF-4N are Strikers. They close.

Swapping a 1N for a 4R does not give you "a Griffin that is a bit different." It changes the shape of the game. The sniper wants you to walk into a firing lane you should have refused. The striker comes and finds you.

The Capellans who bought theirs rather than building them are inside "Inner Sphere General" for most of that run, which is why they do not get a line of their own.

Common questions

What is the best Griffin variant?

In a Succession Wars or 3025 game, the GRF-1N at BV 1272. In the Clan Invasion, the GRF-3M, if you can live with the XL engine. From the Civil War on, the GRF-6S has the better guns and a light engine, and the GRF-3N is the one that kept a standard engine. The Griffin C is the strongest card on the chassis and it costs BV 2131.

Why does the Griffin overheat?

It doesn't, unless you jump and fire both guns together. On the GRF-1N a PPC and an LRM 10 are 14 heat against 12 sinks. A full jump adds another 5. One of those turns puts you up seven. Two of them puts you on fourteen. Fire the LRM while you jump and the PPC once you have stopped, and the machine runs cool.

Is the Griffin a good mech for beginners?

Yes, which is why it is the one I still hand to a new pilot. One long-range gun, one missile launcher, jump jets, and a heat problem that is actually a lesson. It teaches bracketing, positioning and the cost of movement without burying any of that under a twelve-weapon record sheet. Read the heat section once before the first game.

Is the Griffin better than a Shadow Hawk?

It is a better sniper and a worse general medium. The GRF-1N costs BV 1272 against the SHD-2H at 1064, and it buys a long damage bracket of 2 instead of 1. The Shadow Hawk does more up close and costs less. They belong in the same lance. They are not substitutes.

Was the Griffin originally a heavy mech?

Yes. The production model of 2492 was drawn up for a heavy's job and later reclassified as a medium when the weight classes were standardised. The primitive GRF-1A of 2465 really is heavier: sixty tons. Every Griffin after it is fifty-five.

What is the Griffin IIC?

A forty-ton Clan machine introduced in 2852, not a variant of the fifty-five ton Griffin. It keeps the general idea, jump jets and a reach weapon, and shares very little else. It has its own eight variants and it is not ranked in the table on this page.
