Dire Wolf

100 tons · Clan OmniMech · 15 configurations · 3010 to 3142

Clan Wolf's scientist caste conceived it. Clan Smoke Jaguar built it first. My caste does not enjoy telling that story, and I am going to tell it anyway, because it is true and because it explains a great deal about how the touman actually works.

The Jaguars heard what Wolf was developing, called it before the design was finished, and won the plans and the production rights in a Trial that is still spoken of as fought dishonourably. Production began on Huntress in 3010, at the Svarstaad Industriplex Delta, for Smoke Jaguar alone. Clan Wolf did not manufacture their own idea until 3019, on Strana Mechty, when Star Colonel Ulric won a Trial of Possession to take back what his caste had designed.

The Inner Sphere calls it the Daishi. Mangled Japanese for Great Death, and unlike the Mad Cat's name, nobody in authority chose it. It came up through the Draconis Combine's criminal underworld first, and the warriors adopted it afterward because it kept being accurate.

What it is. A hundred tons. Walk three, run five. A Starfire 300 extralight engine carrying the same speed and the same armour class as an Atlas, and roughly fifty tons of free pod space the Atlas does not get. Where an Inner Sphere assault chassis spends its tonnage once, at the factory, this spends it 15 different ways and keeps spending it into the next century.

And what it never got. Not one of those configurations carries a jump jet. Not the first one built in 3010, not the last one built in 3142. A hundred and thirty-two years of Clan engineering, and every warrior who has ever piloted one has done it without leaving the ground.

A note on this one. Clan machines on this site are written from the perspective of a Clan technician rather than the Inner Sphere MechTech who narrates the rest. Same research, same data, different chair.

Why it earns its tonnage

A hundred tons, walk three, run five, matching an Atlas for speed and for armour class down to the point. State that comparison to an Inner Sphere engineer and there is no argument to have, because the numbers agree. What does not agree is the pod space. An Atlas spends its tonnage once, at the factory, on a fixed loadout it will carry for its whole service life. This spends roughly fifty tons of the same hundred on weapons a technician can swap between Trials.

The Prime carries four ER large lasers, twin Ultra AC/5s, four medium pulse lasers and an LRM 10 on twenty-two double heat sinks. On the Alpha Strike card that is 6/6/4 short, medium and long. An AS7-D Atlas manages 5/5/2 on a chassis that cannot change what it is carrying. The Prime is only one of fifteen answers this chassis can give to the same question.

The part that costs something. Fifty tons of free pod space does not arrive without a price, and the price is speed. Walk three is slow even by assault standards, and nothing in fifteen configurations changes it. Every one of them carries the same engine, the same structure, the same armour. What varies is entirely what sits in the pods, which is also why the Trap test on my rating scale finds nothing to flag here. You cannot strictly out-build a Dire Wolf configuration the way you can out-build an Inner Sphere variant. You can only out-manoeuvre one, and at a walking speed of three that is not as difficult as the reputation suggests.

Zero jump jets, stated again because it matters. Every other Clan OmniMech on this site trades some pod space for mobility in at least one configuration. This chassis has never once done that, across a hundred and thirty-two years of production and fifteen separate engineering teams. That is not an oversight repeated fifteen times. It is a design philosophy: the pod space buys firepower, all of it, every time, and the ground it stands on is the only ground it will ever fight from.

A design Clan Wolf did not build first

Clan Wolf's scientist caste designed this as their concept of the ultimate assault OmniMech. Clan Smoke Jaguar heard what was being developed before it was finished, and won the plans and the production rights in a Trial that is, to this day, spoken of as fought dishonourably. Production began at the Svarstaad Industriplex Delta on Huntress in 3010, and for nine years the entire supply belonged to the Jaguars alone.

Clan Wolf did not manufacture their own design until 3019, when Star Colonel Ulric won a Trial of Possession on Strana Mechty and brought production back to the caste that had conceived it. Consider that arrangement for a moment. The Clan that fielded it first, in the opening season of the invasion, was not the Clan that built it.

Wolcott, 2 October 3050, deserves its own mention, because a configuration on this page is named for it. Sho-sa Hohiro Kurita commanded the Combine's on-world defence, and when the Jaguars attacked, he negotiated terms with Galaxy Commander Dietr Osis before the battle was decided: if the Combine held the world, the Clans would never invade Wolcott again, and would provide OmniMechs and Elemental battle armour besides. The Combine held. The (Hohiro) configuration fielded exclusively by Kurita forces is not a coincidence, and neither is the fact that it carries the best long-range damage in the entire chassis.

What followed the invasion is the part my caste finds least comfortable to recount. This machine, designed to be the exclusive property of two Clans, is now fielded by every Clan in the touman and by every Great House of the Inner Sphere besides, ComStar and the Republic included. Salvage, trade and outright capture moved it further and faster than either Wolf or the Jaguars ever intended, and the Dark Age refit built in 3142 proves the design was never allowed to simply age out of relevance.

How these are rated

Every verdict below is against other Dire Wolf configurations. As with every OmniMech on this site, the Trap test finds nothing here: engine, structure and armour are identical across all fifteen, so nothing can be strictly outperformed the way an Inner Sphere variant can be. The tiers are judgement, not arithmetic.

Does the job competently. Another variant does it better or cheaper, but there is no reason to avoid this one.

It is worked out from the variant data rather than decided by me, which means it is falsifiable: to argue with it you only have to name something the cheaper machine gives up. On the table

Fielding one. You are not manoeuvring for position. At walk three you rarely get to choose the range, so choose the configuration for the range you expect instead. The A and Prime punish a straight fight at any distance. The S and W want the enemy close and make them regret arriving. None of the fifteen wants to run anywhere.

Opposing one. Speed is the entire answer. At 2609 to 3610 Battle Value a Dire Wolf costs enough that you will usually outnumber it, and at walk three it cannot chase what it cannot catch. Stay outside its effective bands, force it to commit to a direction, and bring more than one target down that line. The machine that cannot jump cannot correct a mistake in positioning.

What I observe going wrong. Inner Sphere warriors treat the reputation as the machine and close to a range where the reputation is actually earned. It hits hardest at exactly the distance most pilots instinctively want to fight at. Do not give it that distance for free.

Against OpFor Command

If you run solo games with our AI opponent, the Dire Wolf is close to the opposite of the Timber Wolf, and worth fielding for exactly that reason.

  • 13 of the 15 configurations are tagged Juggernaut. Whichever one the engine draws, expect it to close to its effective band and stay there rather than manoeuvre for an angle.
  • No configuration carries a jump jet, so the AI will never take a rooftop or clear a wall the way a Timber Wolf S might. Terrain that blocks a walk-three, run-five machine blocks it completely.
  • Practically, that means this chassis is a straightforward, honest threat rather than a tactical puzzle. Good for a scenario that wants a hard target without a trick to it.

Put a Dire Wolf on the other side of the table when you want raw pressure. Put a Timber Wolf there when you want to be surprised.

Common questions

Why is the Dire Wolf called the Daishi?

Daishi is mangled Japanese for "Great Death," and unlike Mad Cat, no Precentor Martial or official channel assigned it. It surfaced through the Draconis Combine's criminal underworld first and spread from there, because warriors kept finding it accurate.

Did Clan Wolf or Clan Smoke Jaguar build the Dire Wolf?

Clan Wolf's scientist caste designed it, but Clan Smoke Jaguar won the production rights in a Trial fought before the design was finished, and built the first examples on Huntress starting in 3010. Clan Wolf spent nine years building none of their own design before Star Colonel Ulric reclaimed the rights on Strana Mechty.

What is the best Dire Wolf configuration?

The A for a clean, zero-overheat all-rounder, the Widowmaker for the best long-range damage among the early configurations, and the (Hohiro) and D for the best damage-to-heat ratio in the whole chassis. The Prime carries the reputation, but three or four other configurations outperform it on the card.

Does the Dire Wolf have jump jets?

No. Not one of the fifteen configurations, across production from 3010 to 3142, carries a jump jet. The entire pod space budget goes to weapons, every time.

How do you beat a Dire Wolf?

With speed and numbers together. At 2609 to 3610 Battle Value you can usually field more than one machine against it, and at walk three, run five, it cannot chase down anything that keeps its distance. Force it to commit to a direction and answer from more than one angle.
